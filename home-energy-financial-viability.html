A premium, all-electric home energy stack can lower lifetime energy cost, reduce grid risk, and improve resale value — if it is sized and financed correctly.

What You’re Buying

Three systems that work better together

Solar PV

  • Generates low-cost electricity during daytime.
  • Offsets utility purchases and hedges rate inflation.
  • Best economics when self-consumed or net-metered well.

Battery Storage

  • Shifts excess solar into evening peak periods.
  • Enables backup during outages.
  • Can optimize time-of-use tariffs and demand charges.

Heat Pump HVAC / Water Heating

  • Replaces combustion with high-efficiency electrification.
  • Turns solar electricity into heating/cooling value.
  • Reduces gas bill and future carbon compliance risk.

Why the combination matters

  • Heat pump increases electric load that solar can serve.
  • Battery raises self-consumption of PV generation.
  • Together they improve total-system payback stability.

Key Risks

What can erode ROI — and how to protect it

Risk factors

  • Policy change in export compensation or tax credits.
  • Poor system sizing (overproduction, underused battery).
  • Heat pump underperformance due to duct or envelope issues.
  • Installer quality and warranty service reliability.

Mitigations

  • Run hourly interval-data model before signing contract.
  • Require performance guarantees and production modeling assumptions in writing.
  • Bundle envelope upgrades to increase heat pump efficiency.
  • Finance with terms shorter than expected payback where possible.
